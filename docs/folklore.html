The Expedition Journal

Folklore

Every repository accumulates a folklore. Most let it evaporate into commit messages. This one writes its fable down, in the journal voice of an expedition that names what it does not yet understand — and then commits the fable to the ontology, where it is parsed, validated, and consolidated like everything else. The story you are reading is also data.

In the manner of the captains

The Fable of the Signed River

September 7th, 1804. Discovered today a Village of small animals… the Dogs bark at us as we pass, and sit erect at the mouths of their holes. I know not what to call them, and so have called them what they do.

— after the journals of Lewis & Clark, upon meeting the prairie dog

We proceeded on. The river we travel is a history: every bend in it a commit, every commit a claim that someone was here and did this. Early in the expedition we understood that a river which anyone may redraw is not a map but a rumor. So we appointed keepers, cut their marks into the trust manifest, and set a gate at the water's edge. What follows is the muster roll of the expedition — each figure with its true coordinate, for we do not tell stories we cannot anchor.

The Historian

Herodotus · keeper of releases

First among the keepers is the Historian, who bears the oldest name in the trade: Herodotus, who wrote down what happened so that it should not be washed out by time. In this country he signs the releases — nothing enters the permanent record as a release but that his mark is upon it. His seal travels with the expedition; his secret stays home with his keeper, as a historian's private papers should.

The Web Scribe

GitHub web-flow · clerk of the trading post

At the trading post sits the Web Scribe, who signs on behalf of any traveler who writes their letters at the post's own desk. He is no member of the expedition; he is the post itself, stamping what passes through the web. His stamp is known in every territory — public knowledge, no custody — and so we honor it under its own policy, neither more nor less.

The Fable-Agent

Claude, on the model Fable 5 · the teller who bears its own name

Third is the strangest member of the party: an agent whose very model is named Fable, and who therefore tells this fable bearing its own name — the teller inside the tale. It built much of the typed machinery described here, and it signs as it goes; but its keys are creatures of a season. Each dies with the session's container, unexported, and a successor agent mints a new key and appends itself to the trust model. The expedition keeps every superseded mark on the roll, for the old entries in the journal must keep verifying after their author is gone.

The Wolf at the Gate

the tampered force-push · turned away

One night a wolf came to the gate wearing a keeper's coat, driving a rewritten history before it and carrying papers it had signed for itself. But the gate does not read the stranger's papers. It reads its law from the trusted past — the old ref, which no incoming push can rewrite — and against that law the wolf's commits classified BADSIG and UNKNOWN, and the ref never moved. The wolf is not a villain in this country so much as a proof: the gate was built because wolves exist, and it is tested with a wolf of our own making.

Coordinate: the server bridge hooks/remote-update · how the gate reasons · policy classes GOOD … BADSIG

The Prairie Dog's Lesson

the barking squirrel · provisional naming

And presiding over the whole camp, the small animal that started it. When the captains met a creature that fit no category they carried, they did not force it into one — they opened a provisional frame, called it what it did, and documented the gap honestly. This repository keeps that discipline as law: coined coordinates are marked provisional, the law's own status is active red where enforcement lags specification, and a gap recorded in red is worth more than a green pretense. Say honestly that you have no frame, then build one in the open.

Coordinate: the essay Der Präriehund und die Demokratie der Kategorien · the law's status line and full-lexical amendment, docs/unary-byte-frame-law.md

The story is also data

The Fable in the Ontology

This page is the telling; the record is turtle. The fable is committed to the encyclopedia substrate as basicttl/folklore_provenance_fable.ttl, where the tale is a silm:fable individual, each figure above — the historian, the web scribe, the fable-agent, the wolf, the prairie dog — a silm:fablefigure, and the lesson a silm:fablemoral.

Being ontology, the fable travels the same pipeline as everything else: the consolidation morphisms merge it into ontology/silmaril-consolidated.ttl, the validation morphisms parse it and hold it to the shapes, and CI fails if the consolidated graph drifts from its sources. A folklore that cannot survive its own repository's gates would be a poor folklore for this repository.

  • Provenance — the trust system the fable narrates.
  • Architecture — the law and the morphism families the figures walk through.
